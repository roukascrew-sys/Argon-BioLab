# **Technical Literature and Dataset Extraction for an Open-Source Deterministic Type 1 Diabetes ODE Simulator**

Building a deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE) simulator for Type 1 Diabetes (T1D) that couples whole-body meal absorption dynamics with beta-cell replacement kinetics requires verified mathematical formulations, rate constants, and physiological boundary conditions from primary peer-reviewed literature. Across the UVA/Padova metabolic simulator lineage—spanning the foundational meal simulation model of Dalla Man, Rizza, and Cobelli (2007, DOI: 10.1109/TBME.2007.893506), the S2013 regulatory update by Dalla Man et al. (2014, DOI: 10.1177/1932296813514502), and the S2017 single-day diurnal extension by Visentin et al. (2018, PMCID: PMC5851236)—gastro-intestinal carbohydrate transit is governed by a three-compartment system (Q\_{sto1}, Q\_{sto2}, Q\_{gut}) coupled to a biphasic hyperbolic-tangent gastric emptying rate k\_{empt}(Q\_{sto}). While the governing ODEs and default population rate constants (k\_max \= 0.0558 min⁻¹, k\_min \= 0.0080 min⁻¹, k\_abs \= 0.057 min⁻¹, k\_gri \= 0.0558 min⁻¹, f \= 0.90, b \= 0.82, d \= 0.010) are fully specified in primary text and open-source reference code, in-silico T1D adult cohort meal tests (n \= 100 adults ingesting 60 g carbohydrate with optimal insulin bolus) publish discrete numerical benchmarks only for baseline fasting glycemia (119.1 ± 7.1 mg/dL) and meal-specific overbasal glucose peaks (68 ± 30 mg/dL breakfast, 54 ± 27 mg/dL lunch, 53 ± 25 mg/dL dinner), whereas intermediate discrete timepoints (15–240 min) and plasma insulin time series are plotted purely as continuous graphical traces (NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE as discrete tabular numbers).  
For cellular replacement modules modeling unencapsulated hypoimmune islet grafts or macroencapsulated beta-cell devices, survival kinetics are governed by two distinct physical thresholds: an immunological surface-receptor density threshold and a reaction-diffusion oxygen partial pressure boundary. Preclinical and translational studies of the hypoimmune platform (HIP: CRISPR-Cas9/Cas12b knockout of *B2M* and *CIITA* combined with lentiviral *CD47* overexpression) by Deuse et al. (2019, DOI: 10.1038/s41587-019-0016-3), Hu et al. (2023, DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.adg5794; 2024, DOI: 10.1038/s41587-023-01784-x; 2024, DOI: 10.1016/j.stem.2024.02.001), and Carlsson et al. (2025, DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2503822) establish that natural killer (NK) and macrophage evasion requires a strict threshold of 180,000–220,000 CD47 molecules per cell, with human HIP iPSCs and derived islet cells engineered to express \>600,000 CD47 molecules per cell (at viral vector copy number ≥ 2\) to achieve 80%–100% long-term graft survival in humanized mice without immunosuppression. Conversely, finite-element reaction-diffusion models of avascular and hydrogel-encapsulated islets (Buchwald 2009, DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5; Fernandez et al. 2022, DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071, incorporating parameters from Coronel et al. 2017, PMCID: PMC6527135) demonstrate that Michaelis-Menten oxygen consumption (R\_max,islets \= 0.034 mol·s⁻¹·m⁻³, C\_MM \= 0.44–0.70 mmHg) restricts the 48-hour necrotic boundary (0.07–0.10 mmHg O₂) to a radial diffusion distance of 660 µm under normoxia (140 mmHg), 500 µm under arterial tension (76 mmHg), and 200 µm under hypoxia (15 mmHg).  
Every numerical constant, equation, and benchmark below is extracted directly from primary literature with its exact citation, DOI/PMCID, and equation, table, or figure identifier. In strict compliance with deterministic parameterization standards, no missing values have been interpolated or estimated from graphs; parameters or discrete time-series coordinates absent from the primary source text are explicitly marked NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE.

## **1\. Full Gastro-Intestinal Meal Absorption ODEs and Digitized Time-Series Benchmarks**

### **1.1 Exact Three-Compartment Meal Absorption ODEs (UVA/Padova Model)**

In the UVA/Padova glucose-insulin simulator, the gastro-intestinal tract is modeled as a three-compartment dynamic cascade consisting of the solid stomach phase (Q\_{sto1}), the triturated liquid stomach phase (Q\_{sto2}), and the absorbing intestinal gut compartment (Q\_{gut}), which feeds glucose into the plasma compartment (G\_p) via the rate of appearance Ra\_{meal}(t). The exact mathematical formulation is defined in **Equation (13)** of Dalla Man, Rizza, & Cobelli (*IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering*, 2007, Vol. 54(10), pp. 1740–1749, DOI: 10.1109/TBME.2007.893506) and **Appendix Equation (A3)** of Dalla Man et al. (*Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology*, 2014, Vol. 8(1), pp. 26–34, DOI: 10.1177/1932296813514502), as well as the verified open-source TypeScript reference implementation (UvaPadova\_T1DMS.ts):  
\\dot{Q}\_{sto1}(t) \= \-k\_{gri} \\cdot Q\_{sto1}(t) \+ D \\cdot \\delta(t), \\quad Q\_{sto1}(0) \= 0 \\dot{Q}\_{sto2}(t) \= \-k\_{empt}(Q\_{sto}) \\cdot Q\_{sto2}(t) \+ k\_{gri} \\cdot Q\_{sto1}(t), \\quad Q\_{sto2}(0) \= 0 \\dot{Q}\_{gut}(t) \= \-k\_{abs} \\cdot Q\_{gut}(t) \+ k\_{empt}(Q\_{sto}) \\cdot Q\_{sto2}(t), \\quad Q\_{gut}(0) \= 0  
Q\_{sto}(t) \= Q\_{sto1}(t) \+ Q\_{sto2}(t), \\quad Q\_{sto}(0) \= 0 Ra\_{meal}(t) \= \\frac{f \\cdot k\_{abs} \\cdot Q\_{gut}(t)}{BW}, \\quad Ra\_{meal}(0) \= 0  
In **Equation (13)** (DOI: 10.1109/TBME.2007.893506) and **Appendix Equation (A3)** (DOI: 10.1177/1932296813514502), Q\_{sto1}(t) (mg) is the solid glucose mass in the stomach, Q\_{sto2}(t) (mg) is the liquid glucose mass in the stomach, Q\_{gut}(t) (mg) is the glucose mass in the intestine, Q\_{sto}(t) (mg) is the total stomach glucose content, D (mg) is the amount of ingested carbohydrate, \\delta(t) (min⁻¹) is the Dirac impulse function (replaced in continuous-time numerical implementations by the carbohydrate ingestion rate M(t) in mg/min, where M \= \\text{carbs (g)} \\times 1000), BW (kg) is the subject's body weight, f (dimensionless) is the fraction of intestinal absorption that appears in plasma, k\_{gri} (min⁻¹, denoted k\_{21} or k\_{g21} in alternative notations) is the rate constant of stomach grinding/trituration, and k\_{abs} (min⁻¹) is the rate constant of intestinal absorption.  
The nonlinear biphasic gastric emptying rate k\_{empt}(Q\_{sto}) (min⁻¹) is defined in **Equation (13)** of Dalla Man et al. (2007, DOI: 10.1109/TBME.2007.893506) and **Appendix Equation (A3)** of Dalla Man et al. (2014, DOI: 10.1177/1932296813514502) as:  
k\_{empt}(Q\_{sto}) \= k\_{min} \+ \\frac{k\_{max} \- k\_{min}}{2} \\cdot \\left\\{ \\tanh\\left\[\\alpha \\cdot (Q\_{sto}(t) \- b \\cdot D)\\right\] \- \\tanh\\left\[\\beta \\cdot (Q\_{sto}(t) \- d \\cdot D)\\right\] \+ 2 \\right\\}  
where the steepness coefficients \\alpha (mg⁻¹) and \\beta (mg⁻¹) are algebraically constrained by the meal dose D and the fractional thresholds b and d (note: parameter d in Dalla Man et al. 2007 is denoted c or cmeal in Dalla Man et al. 2006 and the 2014 simulator code):  
\\alpha \= \\frac{5}{2 \\cdot D \\cdot (1 \- b)}, \\quad \\beta \= \\frac{5}{2 \\cdot D \\cdot d}  
In the 2014 multi-meal simulator specification (**Appendix Equation (A3)** of Dalla Man et al., 2014, DOI: 10.1177/1932296813514502, as implemented in UvaPadova\_T1DMS.ts), a constant reference meal amount \\bar{D} \= 30,000\\text{ mg} (30 g) and two zero-derivative memory states (MealMemory and QstoMemory) are introduced to normalize gastric emptying across variable or overlapping meal sizes:

\\tilde{Q}\_{sto}(t) \= (Q\_{sto1}(t) \+ Q\_{sto2}(t)) \\cdot \\frac{\\bar{D}}{D \+ Q\_{sto,\\text{memory}}}, \\quad \\alpha \= \\frac{5}{2 \\cdot \\bar{D} \\cdot (1 \- b)}, \\quad \\beta \= \\frac{5}{2 \\cdot \\bar{D} \\cdot c} k\_{empt}(\\tilde{Q}\_{sto}) \= \\left( k\_{min} \+ \\frac{k\_{max} \- k\_{min}}{2} \\cdot \\left\\{ \\tanh\\left\[\\alpha \\cdot (\\tilde{Q}\_{sto} \- b \\cdot \\bar{D})\\right\] \- \\tanh\\left\[\\beta \\cdot (\\tilde{Q}\_{sto} \- c \\cdot \\bar{D})\\right\] \+ 2 \\right\\} \\right) \\cdot \\max\\left(1, \\frac{\\bar{D}}{D}\\right)  
*(Note: While Dalla Man et al. (2014) Appendix Eq. (A3) introduces multi-meal memory states (D\_memory / Q\_sto\_memory), the \\bar{D} \= 30,000 mg normalization and \\max(1, \\bar{D}/D) scaling factor are specific to the UvaPadova\_T1DMS.ts reference implementation.)*

### **1.2 Coupled Whole-Body Glucose-Insulin ODE Context**

To integrate Ra\_{meal}(t) into a deterministic T1D simulator, the open-source UVA/Padova implementation (UvaPadova\_T1DMS.ts) couples **Appendix Equation (A3)** to the complete glucose-insulin system defined in **Equations (1), (3), (8), (10), (11), (14), (18), and (27)** of Dalla Man et al. (2007, DOI: 10.1109/TBME.2007.893506) and **Appendix Equations (A1)–(A17)** of Dalla Man et al. (2014, DOI: 10.1177/1932296813514502):

\\dot{G}\_p(t) \= EGP(t) \+ Ra\_{meal}(t) \- U\_{ii}(t) \- E(t) \- k\_1 \\cdot G\_p(t) \+ k\_2 \\cdot G\_t(t), \\quad G(t) \= \\frac{G\_p(t)}{V\_G} \\dot{G}\_t(t) \= \-U\_{id}(t) \+ k\_1 \\cdot G\_p(t) \- k\_2 \\cdot G\_t(t), \\quad \\dot{G}\_s(t) \= \\frac{G\_p(t) \- G\_s(t)}{T\_d} EGP(t) \= \\max\\left(0, k\_{p1} \- k\_{p2} \\cdot G\_p(t) \- k\_{p3} \\cdot X\_L(t) \+ k\_{\\xi} \\cdot X\_H(t)\\right), \\quad U\_{ii}(t) \= F\_{cns} U\_{id}(t) \= \\frac{\\left\[V\_{m0} \+ V\_{mx} \\cdot X(t) \\cdot (1 \+ r\_{\\gamma} \\cdot \\text{risk})\\right\] \\cdot G\_t(t)}{K\_{m0} \+ G\_t(t)}, \\quad E(t) \= \\max\\left(k\_{e1} \\cdot (G\_p(t) \- k\_{e2}), 0\\right) \\dot{I}\_p(t) \= \-(m\_2 \+ m\_4) \\cdot I\_p(t) \+ m\_1 \\cdot I\_l(t) \+ Ra\_I(t), \\quad \\dot{I}\_l(t) \= m\_2 \\cdot I\_p(t) \- (m\_1 \+ m\_{3,eq}) \\cdot I\_l(t), \\quad I(t) \= \\frac{I\_p(t)}{V\_I} \\dot{I}\_{sc1}(t) \= \-(k\_d \+ k\_{a1}) \\cdot I\_{sc1}(t) \+ \\frac{IIR(t)}{BW}, \\quad \\dot{I}\_{sc2}(t) \= k\_d \\cdot I\_{sc1}(t) \- k\_{a2} \\cdot I\_{sc2}(t), \\quad Ra\_I(t) \= k\_{a1} \\cdot I\_{sc1}(t) \+ k\_{a2} \\cdot I\_{sc2}(t) \\dot{I}'(t) \= \-k\_i \\cdot (I'(t) \- I(t)), \\quad \\dot{X}\_L(t) \= \-k\_i \\cdot (X\_L(t) \- I'(t)), \\quad \\dot{X}(t) \= \-p\_{2U} \\cdot X(t) \+ p\_{2U} \\cdot (I(t) \- I\_b)

### **1.2.1 Endogenous / Graft Beta-Cell Insulin Secretion Submodel (Dalla Man et al., 2007, Eqs. 7–9)**

To incorporate endogenous pancreatic secretion or transplanted beta-cell graft secretion into the continuous-time simulator, portal/graft secretion S(t) is modeled via the dynamic equations from Dalla Man et al. (2007, DOI: 10.1109/TBME.2007.893506, Eqs. 7–9):  
\\dot{I}\_{po}(t) \= \-\\gamma \\cdot I\_{po}(t) \+ S\_{po}(t), \\quad I\_{po}(0) \= I\_{pob} S(t) \= \\gamma \\cdot I\_{po}(t) S\_{po}(t) \= \\begin{cases} Y(t) \+ K \\cdot \\dot{G}(t) \+ S\_b, & \\text{if } \\dot{G}(t) \> 0 \\\\ Y(t) \+ S\_b, & \\text{if } \\dot{G}(t) \\le 0 \\end{cases} \\dot{Y}(t) \= \\begin{cases} \-\\alpha\_{sec} \\cdot \[Y(t) \- \\beta\_{sec} \\cdot (G(t) \- h)\], & \\text{if } \\beta\_{sec} \\cdot (G(t) \- h) \\ge \-S\_b \\\\ \-\\alpha\_{sec} \\cdot Y(t) \- \\alpha\_{sec} \\cdot S\_b, & \\text{if } \\beta\_{sec} \\cdot (G(t) \- h) \< \-S\_b \\end{cases}  
For portal or intrahepatic vascular graft placements, secretion feeds into liver kinetics via \+ S(t) added to \\dot{I}\_l(t), with hepatic extraction m\_3(t) \= \\frac{HE(t) \\cdot m\_1}{1 \- HE(t)}. For subcutaneous, intramuscular, or hydrogel-macroencapsulated graft sites bypassing first-pass hepatic extraction, viable graft secretion feeds directly into systemic plasma via \+ f\_{viable}(t) \\cdot S(t) added to \\dot{I}\_p(t). Default population parameters from Dalla Man et al. (2007, Table I) are: \\gamma \= 0.5\\text{ min}^{-1}, K \= 2.30\\text{ pmol}\\cdot\\text{kg}^{-1}/(\\text{mg/dL}), \\alpha\_{sec} \= 0.050\\text{ min}^{-1}, \\beta\_{sec} \= 0.11\\text{ pmol}\\cdot\\text{kg}^{-1}\\cdot\\text{min}^{-1}/(\\text{mg/dL}), and h \= G\_b\\text{ mg/dL}.  
*(Note: In the coupled system, X\_H(t) (glucagon action), k\_{\\xi}, m\_{3,eq} / HE(t), and hypoglycemic risk(G) are defined in Subsection 1.2.2 \[Dalla Man et al. (2014) Appendix Eqs. (A7)–(A17)\]. Standard UVA/Padova unit conversion enforces 1 µU/mL \= 6.0 pmol/L.)*

### **1.2.2 Hepatic Extraction, Glucagon Kinetics, and Hypoglycemic Risk Submodels (Dalla Man et al., 2014, Appendix Eqs. A7–A17)**

1. **Hepatic Insulin Extraction HE(t) and Liver Clearance Rate m\_3(t) (Appendix Eqs. A7–A8; Dalla Man et al., 2007, Eqs. 4–5):**

HE(t) \= \-m\_5 \\cdot S(t) \+ m\_6, \\quad HE(0) \= HE\_b m\_3(t) \= \\frac{HE(t) \\cdot m\_1}{1 \- HE(t)}  
In untreated T1D (S(t) \= 0), HE(t) \= m\_6 \= HE\_b and m\_3(t) reduces to the constant equilibrium clearance m\_{3,eq} \= \\frac{m\_6 \\cdot m\_1}{1 \- m\_6}. When an intrahepatic/portal beta-cell graft secretes S(t) \> 0, hepatic extraction decreases linearly with S(t) via slope m\_5 (dimensionless: kg·min·pmol⁻¹).

2. **Hypoglycemic Risk Modulation of Insulin-Dependent Utilization (Appendix Eq. A12):**

In U\_{id}(t), insulin action X(t) is amplified in hypoglycemia by (1 \+ r\_{\\gamma} \\cdot \\text{risk}), where the piecewise risk function is:  
\\text{risk} \= \\begin{cases} 0, & \\text{if } G(t) \\ge G\_b \\\\ 10 \\cdot \[f(G(t))\]^2, & \\text{if } G\_{th} \\le G(t) \< G\_b \\\\ 10 \\cdot \[f(G\_{th})\]^2, & \\text{if } G(t) \< G\_{th} \\end{cases}  
with hypoglycemic clamp threshold G\_{th} \= 60\\text{ mg/dL} and Kovatchev logarithmic symmetrization f(G) \= (\\ln G)^{r\_1} \- r\_2 (where r\_1 \= 1.084 and r\_2 \= (\\ln G\_b)^{r\_1}, with r\_2 \= 5.381 for reference G\_b \= 112.5\\text{ mg/dL}).

3. **Glucagon Kinetics, Secretion, and Action on EGP (Appendix Eqs. A13–A17):**

Plasma glucagon compartment (H(t) in pg/mL, Eq. A13):  
\\dot{H}(t) \= \-n \\cdot H(t) \+ SR\_H(t) \+ Ra\_H(t), \\quad H(0) \= H\_b  
where n (min⁻¹) is plasma glucagon clearance, SR\_H(t) \= SR\_H^s(t) \+ SR\_H^d(t) (pg·mL⁻¹·min⁻¹) is endogenous alpha-cell glucagon secretion, and Ra\_H(t) is exogenous subcutaneous glucagon appearance (0 in standard simulations without glucagon rescue).  
Delayed glucagon action on EGP (X\_H(t) in pg/mL, Eq. A14):  
\\dot{X}\_H(t) \= \-k\_H \\cdot X\_H(t) \+ k\_H \\cdot \\max(H(t) \- H\_b, 0), \\quad X\_H(0) \= 0  
which enters EGP(t) via \+ k\_{\\xi} \\cdot X\_H(t), where k\_H (min⁻¹) is the glucagon action delay rate and k\_{\\xi} (mg·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹/(pg/mL)) is liver sensitivity to glucagon.  
Static glucagon secretion (SR\_H^s(t), Eqs. A15–A16):  
\\dot{SR}\_H^s(t) \= \\begin{cases} \-\\rho \\cdot \\left\[ SR\_H^s(t) \- \\max\\left(\\sigma\_2 \\cdot (G\_{th} \- G(t)) \+ SR\_{Hb}^s, 0\\right) \\right\], & \\text{if } G(t) \\ge G\_b \\\\ \-\\rho \\cdot \\left\[ SR\_H^s(t) \- \\max\\left(\\frac{\\sigma}{\\max(I(t) \- I\_{th}, 0\) \+ 1} \\cdot (G\_{th} \- G(t)) \+ SR\_{Hb}^s, 0\\right) \\right\], & \\text{if } G(t) \< G\_b \\end{cases}  
with basal secretion SR\_{Hb}^s \= n \\cdot H\_b, insulin inhibition threshold I\_{th} \= I\_b, static delay rate \\rho (min⁻¹), and glucose-responsivity slopes \\sigma and \\sigma\_2.  
Dynamic glucagon secretion (SR\_H^d(t), Eq. A17):  
SR\_H^d(t) \= \\delta\_H \\cdot \\max(-\\dot{G}(t), 0\)  
where \\delta\_H ((pg/mL)/(mg/dL)) is glucagon responsivity to falling plasma glucose (\\dot{G}(t) \< 0).  
*(Note: Individual virtual patient parameter distributions for the S2013 glucagon and risk submodels are sampled from the UVA/Padova cohort covariance matrix rather than published as static scalar population means in the main text of DOI: 10.1177/1932296813514502; parameters are explicitly marked NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE as a static scalar table in the 2014 main text.)*

## **2\. Quantitative Hypoimmune Islet Immune-Evasion and Encapsulation Survival Data (2019–2026)**

### **2.1 Hypoimmune Stem-Cell-Derived and Primary Islet Studies (Surface Expression, Cytotoxicity Kinetics, and Graft Survival)**

Across the 2019–2026 literature, genetically engineered immune evasion for beta-cell replacement has centered on the Hypoimmune Platform (HIP)—developed by Deuse, Hu, Schrepfer, and colleagues at UCSF and Sana Biotechnology (preclinical stem-cell-derived islet candidate **SC451** and clinical primary-islet candidate **UP421**)—alongside alternative checkpoint knock-in strategies utilizing single-chain **B2M–HLA-E**, **B2M–HLA-G1**, or inducible **PD-L1**.  
In the HIP architecture, CRISPR-Cas9 (or CRISPR-Cas12b in clinical UP421 manufacturing) is used to ablate the \\beta\_2-microglobulin (*B2M*) gene—eliminating cell-surface HLA class I expression and preventing direct CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell recognition—and the class II major histocompatibility complex transactivator (*CIITA*) gene, abolishing constitutive and cytokine-inducible HLA class II expression to prevent direct CD4+ T-cell activation. Because complete loss of HLA class I triggers innate natural killer (NK) cell and macrophage killing via "missing-self" recognition, HIP cells are transduced with a lentiviral transgene overexpressing **CD47**, which binds the inhibitory receptor **SIRPα** on NK cells and macrophages.

#### **Quantitative Surface Expression and Molecular Thresholds**

In **Deuse et al. (2019)** (*Nature Biotechnology* 37(3):252–258, DOI: 10.1038/s41587-019-0016-3, PMCID: PMC6419516, **Figure 2b** and **Supplementary Figure 11d–f, i–j**), CRISPR-Cas9 inactivation of *B2M* and *CIITA* in human iPSCs (B2M^{-/-}CIITA^{-/-}) and mouse iPSCs (B2m^{-/-}Ciita^{-/-}) yielded a complete lack (100% surface depletion after flow-cytometric sorting) of HLA class I/II (or MHC class I/II) molecules, although exact numerical fold-change ratios for B2M, CIITA, and CD47 surface expression are NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE. Similarly, in **Hu et al. (2023)** (*Science Translational Medicine* 15(691):eadg5794, DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.adg5794, **Figure 1A, 1C**, **Figure 2G**, **Figure 5B–E**, and **Supplementary Figure S1B**), primary human and mouse islets were dissociated and edited via CRISPR-Cas9 (mouse gRNAs: *B2m* 5′\-TTCGGCTTCCCATTCTCCGG-3′, *Ciita* 5′\-GGTCCATCTGGTCATAGAGG-3′), sorted for HLA class I and II negativity, and transduced with lentiviral CD47 and firefly luciferase vectors at a **multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 20**. The resulting HIP pseudoislets (p-islets) remained completely HLA class I–negative and HLA class II–negative even after inflammatory stimulation with IFN-γ and TNF-α (**Supplementary Figure S1B**), whereas raw pre-sorting knockout efficiency percentages are NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE.  
While **Deuse et al. (2019)** (DOI: 10.1038/s41587-019-0016-3, **Figure 2b**) and **Deuse et al. (2021)** (*Journal of Experimental Medicine* 218(3):e20200839, DOI: 10.1084/jem.20200839, **Figure 2G**) reported representative flow-cytometry histograms of CD47 overexpression without numerical fold-change values (NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE), **Hu et al. (2024)** (*Nature Biotechnology* 42(3):413–423, DOI: 10.1038/s41587-023-01784-x, PMCID: PMC10940156) quantified the exact receptor counts per cell required for innate immune evasion. Specifically, the minimum protective threshold to prevent NK-cell killing is **180,000–220,000 CD47 molecules per cell**, below which cells undergo rapid lysis, whereas engineered human HIP iPSCs and derived islet cells (the preclinical **SC451** platform) express **\>600,000 CD47 molecules per cell** at a lentiviral **vector copy number (VCN) of ≥ 2**, conferring complete innate immune protection.  
For alternative immune-checkpoint modifications, **Santini-González et al. (2022)** (PMCID: PMC9732725) engineered human stem-cell-derived beta-like cells (sBCs) with inducible surface **PD-L1** expression in the presence (iP) or absence (iP-BKO, *B2M* knockout) of HLA class I, demonstrating maximal PD-L1 surface induction under combined inflammatory cytokine treatment and improved transplant survival in humanized HLA class I NOD mice, though exact numerical fold-change values for PD-L1 surface expression are NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE. In parallel strategies reviewed in recent literature (*Frontiers in Immunology* 2025/2026; PMCID: PMC12492583, PMCID: PMC12401705), AAV-mediated knock-in of a single-chain **B2M–HLA-E** fusion protein at the *B2M* locus binds the NK inhibitory receptor NKG2A, evading CD8+ T cells, diminishing NK lysis, and enhancing survival of derived CD45+ cells over **3 days** in NK-reconstituted immunodeficient mice. Similarly, knock-in of **B2M–HLA-G1** at the *B2M* locus reduced T-cell and NK cytotoxicity and decreased NK cell infiltration **20 days** after subcutaneous transplantation in humanized mice, with exact numerical surface expression fold-changes NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE.

#### **Quantitative NK-Cell, Macrophage, and CD8+ T-Cell Cytotoxicity Assay Kinetics**

In xCELLigence real-time electrical impedance cytotoxicity assays run over **90 hours** in **Hu et al. (2024)** (DOI: 10.1038/s41587-023-01784-x, PMCID: PMC10940156), target cell killing kinetics are quantified by the percentage of target cell killing at 90 hours and the killing speed, defined as:  
\\text{Killing Speed} \= \\left(\\text{Time to reach Cell Index of } 0.5\\right)^{-1}  
Primary administration of wild-type (wt^xeno) cells elicited peak T-cell activation with IFN-γ ELISpot frequencies of **\~600 spots on day 7**, driving rapid in-vitro PBMC and T-cell killing that declined over **75 days** (with re-injection on day 75 triggering a secondary recall response of similar magnitude and kinetics), while serum total IgM peaked early at **day 7** and total IgG peaked at **day 13**. In contrast, HIP^xeno cells injected into pre-sensitized hosts provoked **0 cellular activation** and **0 killing** by NK cells or macrophages, whereas DKO (B2M^{-/-}CIITA^{-/-}) cells underwent rapid NK and macrophage lysis.  
In human NK cell ELISpot assays reported in **Deuse et al. (2019)** (DOI: 10.1038/s41587-019-0016-3, **Figure 2i**), unprotected B2M^{-/-}CIITA^{-/-} hiPSCs triggered human NK cell IFN-γ release reaching **\~1/3 (33%) of the spot frequency** of the highly NK-susceptible K562 control cell line, whereas B2M^{-/-}CIITA^{-/-}\\text{ CD47 tg} hiPSCs induced **0% measurable IFN-γ response**. Furthermore, **Deuse et al. (2019)** (**Supplementary Figure 5d–h**) and **Deuse et al. (2021)** (DOI: 10.1084/jem.20200839, **Figure 2L–R**) showed that syngeneic C57BL/6 mouse NK cells rapidly killed B2m^{-/-}Ciita^{-/-} mouse endothelial cells (miECs) but spared WT and B2m^{-/-}Ciita^{-/-}\\text{ Cd47 tg} miECs (**Supplementary Fig. 5d, 5e**). Blocking mouse Cd47 with an antibody (**Supplementary Fig. 5f**), knocking out host *Sirpa* (Sirpa^{-/-} NK cells and macrophages, **Fig. 2L–R** of DOI: 10.1084/jem.20200839), or exposing mouse B2m^{-/-}Ciita^{-/-}\\text{ Cd47 tg} miECs to human NK cells (**Supplementary Fig. 5g**) resulted in rapid target cell killing, demonstrating that CD47–SIRPα protection is species-specific.

#### **Governing Oxygen Convection-Diffusion-Reaction ODEs/PDEs**

In both **Buchwald (2009)** (DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5, **Equations 1–4**) and **Fernandez et al. (2022)** (DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071, **Equations 1–3**), oxygen concentration c\_{O\_2} (mol·m⁻³) within avascular islet tissue and alginate encapsulation matrices is governed by the coupled convection-diffusion-reaction partial differential equation solved via finite-element modeling in COMSOL Multiphysics:  
\\nabla \\cdot \\left(-D\_{eff} \\nabla c\_{O\_2} \+ \\mathbf{u} c\_{O\_2}\\right) \= R \\quad \\text{\[Eq. 1 in DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071; Eq. 1 in DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5\]} R \= \-\\frac{R\_{max,O\_2} \\cdot c\_{O\_2}}{K\_{MM,O\_2} \+ c\_{O\_2}} \\cdot H\_{smooth}(c\_{O\_2} \- c\_{cr}) \\cdot X \\quad \\text{\[Eq. 2 in DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071; Eq. 2 in DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5\]} D\_{eff} \= (1 \- X) D\_{alg} \+ X D\_{tissue} \\quad \\text{\[Eq. 3 in DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071\]}  
In **Buchwald (2009)** (DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5), the smoothed Heaviside step-down function H\_{smooth}(c\_{O\_2} \- C\_{cr}) that zeroes oxygen consumption below the critical necrotic concentration C\_{cr} \= 1.0 \\times 10^{-4}\\text{ mol}\\cdot\\text{m}^{-3} (0.07 mmHg) is implemented as H\_{smooth}(c) \= \\text{flc1hs}(c \- 1.0 \\times 10^{-4}, 0.5 \\times 10^{-4}). Note that aqueous oxygen concentration converts to partial pressure via Henry's law solubility \\alpha\_{O\_2} \\approx 1.414 \\times 10^{-3}\\text{ mol}\\cdot\\text{m}^{-3}\\cdot\\text{mmHg}^{-1} at 37°C. Furthermore, **Equation (3)** of **Buchwald (2009)** introduces a Michaelis-Menten glucose dependency scaling the consumption rate with C\_{MM,gluc} \= 8\\text{ mol}\\cdot\\text{m}^{-3} (8 mM) and scaling factor \\varphi \= 3.67, increasing R\_{max} from 0.034 mol·s⁻¹·m⁻³ at low (3 mM) glucose to 0.074 mol·s⁻¹·m⁻³ at high (11 mM) glucose, while **Equation (4)** models the surrounding fluid velocity field \\mathbf{u} via the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations:

\\rho \\left( \\frac{\\partial \\mathbf{u}}{\\partial t} \+ \\mathbf{u} \\cdot \\nabla \\mathbf{u} \\right) \= \-\\nabla p \+ \\eta \\nabla^2 \\mathbf{u} \+ \\mathbf{F}, \\quad \\nabla \\cdot \\mathbf{u} \= 0 \\quad \\text{\[Eq. 4 in DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5\]}

#### **Experimental and Modeled Islet Viability Across Capsule/Radial Thicknesses (100 µm to 500 µm) and Timepoints (24h, 48h, 72h)**

A critical structural distinction exists between how **Buchwald (2009)** (DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5) and **Fernandez et al. (2022)** (DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071) report viability across the 100–500 µm length scale and 24h–72h time window. **Buchwald (2009)** (DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5) models avascular spherical pancreatic islets of **100 µm, 150 µm, and 200 µm diameter** in static culture (**Figure 1**), on a **275 µm (0.275 mm) thick oxygen-permeable silicone membrane** at seeding densities up to **600 IEQ/cm²** (**Figure 4 / Additional File 4** and **Figure 5**), and in dynamic perifusion chambers at 3 mM vs. 11 mM glucose (**Figures 6 and 7**). On the 275 µm oxygen-permeable silicone membrane at up to **600 IEQ/cm²** (**Figure 4 and Figure 5**) and under 3 mM low-glucose perifusion (**Figure 6 and Figure 7**), all regions of the islets across all three diameters (100, 150, and 200 µm) remain well above the critical necrotic threshold (C\_{cr} \= 0.07 mmHg, yielding **0% necrosis**). Because Buchwald (2009) is a steady-state and perifusion finite-element modeling paper, discrete experimental viability percentages at **24h, 48h, and 72h** across 100–500 µm hydrogel capsule thicknesses are NOT REPORTED IN SOURCE.  
Conversely, **Fernandez et al. (2022)** (DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071, incorporating Coronel et al. 2017\) evaluates oxygen transport across radial hydrogel distances of **0 µm, 250 µm, and 500 µm** from the perfusion channel (**Figure 3** and *Materials and Methods*) across variations in cell volume fraction X (**Figure 3C**, spanning X \= 0.010 for 1,000 ILCs/mL up to X \= 0.058 for human-scale 600,000 IEQ in 18 mL) and R\_{max} (**Figure 3D**), alongside **48-hour perfusion culture experiments** (**Figure 4A–C**) at a seeding density of **1,000 MIN6 ILCs/mL gel** (X \= 0.010, 1,000 cells/ILC, mean cluster diameter 269 µm) under three oxygen tensions. Under **Normoxia (140 mmHg O₂ / 18.6% O₂ at 48h, Figure 4\)**, histologically intact (Int) and early degenerative (ED) clusters survive over a radial distance of **\>5 mm (\>5,000 µm)** from the channel, with the 7 mmHg O₂ functional contour at **0.45 mm (450 µm)** and the 0.1 mmHg O₂ necrosis contour at **0.66 mm (660 µm)**, keeping cells at the 0 µm, 250 µm, and 500 µm radial positions above the necrotic threshold. Under **Arterial Oxygen (76 mmHg O₂ / 10% O₂ at 48h, Figure 4\)**, intact and ED cluster distribution is restricted to **\<2 mm (\<2,000 µm)**, with the 7 mmHg O₂ functional threshold shifting inward to **0.25 mm (250 µm)** and the 0.1 mmHg O₂ necrosis boundary shifting to **0.50 mm (500 µm)**. Under **Hypoxia (15 mmHg O₂ / 2% O₂ at 48h, Figure 4\)**, intact clusters are severely restricted to **\<0.6 mm (\<600 µm)**, with the 7 mmHg O₂ functional boundary collapsing to **\~0 mm (0 µm)** and the 0.1 mmHg O₂ necrosis boundary occurring at **0.20 mm (200 µm)**, placing hydrogel regions at 250 µm and 500 µm entirely below the necrotic threshold. ## **4\. Synthesis for Deterministic Simulator Integration**

Unifying these three domains into a single deterministic ODE simulation pipeline requires coupling whole-body meal absorption, radial graft oxygen transport, and surface-marker-gated immune clearance. First, for meal absorption and systemic glycemia, the simulator should implement the corrected mass-conserving 15-state ODE system defined in Sections 1.1–1.2 (incorporating the endogenous/graft beta-cell secretion submodel from Section 1.2.1; Dalla Man et al. 2007, 2014), using \\bar{D} \= 30,000\\text{ mg} and MealMemory/QstoMemory to ensure smooth hyperbolic-tangent transitions in k\_{empt}(Q\_{sto}) across multiple daily meals, while validating solver output against the adult T1D cohort fasting glucose benchmark (119.1 ± 7.1 mg/dL) and 60 g CHO overbasal peak benchmarks (68 ± 30 mg/dL breakfast, 54 ± 27 mg/dL lunch, 53 ± 25 mg/dL dinner) from **Table 1** and **Figure 4** of PMCID: PMC5851236.  
Second, for macroencapsulated beta-cell replacement modules, solving the 1D/2D radial reaction-diffusion oxygen PDE (**Equations 1–3** of DOI: 10.3389/fbioe.2022.884071) requires spatial discretization (or a steady-state analytical/quasi-steady-state radial profile lookup table) coupled to the time-domain glucose ODEs via the Michaelis-Menten glucose-dependent oxygen consumption term R\_{max}(G) (with glucose converted from mg/dL to mM via 1 mM \= 18.018 mg/dL, and oxygen converted from mol·m⁻³ to mmHg via Henry's solubility constant \\alpha\_{O\_2} \\approx 1.414 \\times 10^{-3}\\text{ mol}\\cdot\\text{m}^{-3}\\cdot\\text{mmHg}^{-1}). Parameterized with D\_{alg} \= 2.54 × 10⁻⁵ cm²·s⁻¹, D\_{tissue} \= 1.24 × 10⁻⁵ cm²·s⁻¹, K\_{MM,O\_2} \= 0.62 µM (0.44 mmHg), and R\_{max}(G) scaling from 0.034 to 0.074 mol·s⁻¹·m⁻³ (**Equation 3** of DOI: 10.1186/1742-4682-6-5), applying the necrotic step function at c\_{cr} \= 0.07–0.10 mmHg and attenuating insulin secretion below the functional contour of 7 mmHg O₂ restricts viable non-oxygenated hydrogel half-thickness to ≤250–500 µm under arterial oxygen (76 mmHg) and \<200 µm under hypoxia (15 mmHg).

Third, for unencapsulated hypoimmune (HIP) islet grafts (B2M^{-/-}CIITA^{-/-}\\text{ CD47+}), parameterizing innate NK-cell and macrophage clearance as a step function gated by surface CD47 density is a deterministic modeling abstraction derived from the empirical survival thresholds in Hu et al. (2024a, DOI: 10.1038/s41587-023-01784-x): rapid lysis (matching DKO graft rejection within 7–10 days in **Figure 2H–I** of DOI: 10.1126/scitranslmed.adg5794) when CD47 \< 180,000 molecules/cell, transitioning to 0% innate/adaptive cytotoxicity and 80%–100% graft survival when CD47 \> 220,000 molecules/cell (with preclinical and clinical HIP islets operating at \>600,000 molecules/cell). Clinical UP421 dosing comes from Carlsson et al. (2025, DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2503822; full reference dose \= 11,547 ± 1,604 IEQ/kg at 1,560 cells/IEQ; 7.1% administered in the first-in-human trial).
